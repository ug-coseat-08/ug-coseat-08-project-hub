The following is an editorial outline, not completed research or approved scope. Each point needs the team's verified source and conclusion. Suggested length is roughly 200–300 words per product, subject to the actual assessment brief; this is not a course word limit.

**ConnectMyTask — research aim:** Determine how the approved marketplace workflows can be improved without weakening payment correctness, user trust or existing integrations.

- **Payout correctness and recovery:** Review existing payout states and unit-test limitations. Investigate duplicate requests, rejection, interrupted processing and recovery using synthetic data. Expected output: a reviewed state/behaviour matrix and test evidence linked to CMT-56 and any approved CMT-59 work.
- **Country and payment configuration:** Identify the client decisions needed for supported countries, currencies, forms and gateways. Expected output: a reviewed country/payment matrix linked to CMT-30 and CMT-57. Provider examples remain candidates until confirmed.
- **Integration and deployment reliability:** Summarise configuration, transport and dependency findings. Propose checks for the accepted web/API workflows and, conditionally, mobile. Expected output: repeatable setup, integration results and release-readiness evidence tied to the approved backlog.

**CuponZ — research aim:** Determine how to preserve its lead-generation purpose while making the approved application components easier to run, test and maintain.

- **Lead generation and data access:** Confirm the customer-to-shop-owner journey, subscription/admin rules, permitted data access and retention decisions. Expected output: a client-reviewed journey and role/data matrix. Link FUNC-003/FUNC-004 and the relevant agreed delivery tickets; do not invent missing ticket IDs.
- **Repeatable setup:** Summarise the dated setup failures and compare a documented supported environment with containerised setup. Expected output: a clean-checkout setup check and configuration guide, linked to CZ-6/CZ-7/CZ-10/CZ-32.
- **Modernisation approach:** Compare retaining and decoupling the current system with a limited replacement approach against behaviour preservation, effort, migration risk and team capability. Expected output: a reviewed architecture decision from CZ-20 before any CZ-21/CZ-23 implementation.
- **Security and regression evidence:** Use the dated advisory baseline to prioritise remediation, alongside the separate credential-response action. Expected output: triage evidence and tests for approved customer, shop-owner and administrator journeys, linked to CZ-11–17 and any additional reviewed work.

For the final text, use this pattern for each point: **Finding → source → implication for this product → proposed investigation/output → status/ticket**. Research explains the evidence behind a choice; the solution section records the choice; the backlog records the task. Avoid repeating the same paragraph in all three places.

### Practical standards and acceptance examples

Use [ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html) as a requirements-engineering reference and [ISO/IEC 25010:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78176.html) as a product-quality reference. Describe the selected practices and how they will be used; citing a standard does not establish compliance.

The following are **proposed examples for discussion**, not agreed requirements:

- **ConnectMyTask payout integrity:** Repeated or concurrent submission of the same withdrawal request must not create duplicate payment effects. Record the fixture, resulting states and independent review.
- **CuponZ lead access:** An unauthorised shop account must not retrieve another shop's customer contact data through the agreed UI/API paths. Record negative access tests against the approved role matrix.
- **Deployment:** A second member can follow the guide from a clean checkout and complete the approved staging deployment and smoke checks without undocumented steps.
- **Regression testing:** Each accepted critical journey has recorded expected results and execution by someone other than its implementer; unresolved release-blocking defects prevent a positive readiness recommendation.

Availability, performance, retention and recovery targets need the relevant client decisions and test conditions. Avoid inserting arbitrary percentages or response times merely to make the document look measurable.

For ethics, distinguish good-practice proposals from confirmed legal applicability. The [OAIC's coverage guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-legislation/the-privacy-act/rights-and-responsibilities) explains that Privacy Act responsibilities depend on the organisation's circumstances; the team's Australian location alone does not settle the client's obligations. A hosted payment provider also does not eliminate all merchant responsibilities: see the [PCI SSC explanation](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/does-pci-dss-apply-to-merchants-who-outsource-all-payment-processing-operations-and-never-store-process-or-transmit-cardholder-data/). Record the applicable jurisdictions, provider/integration boundary and policy questions for qualified client review.
